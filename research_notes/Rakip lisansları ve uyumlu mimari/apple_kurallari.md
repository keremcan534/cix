# Apple App Store rules for social voice / live apps with lucky gifts, random rewards, in-room chance games and creator cash-outs (Nov 2026)

Scope: Apple only. Builds on phase 1 (`research_notes/Rakip analizi ve yasal yayın stratejisi/magaza_politikalari.md`); clause texts already quoted there are only re-quoted where the analysis needs the exact words. All sources read on 25 Nov 2026.

Versions recorded:
- App Review Guidelines: page stamp "Last Updated: October 16, 2026" — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). The 16 Oct 2026 change note lists only 1.2, 2.5.19 and Section 5 — [Apple Developer News, 16 Oct 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=vvzx6cul).
- Apple Developer Program License Agreement (DPLA): footer "LYL262 / October 29, 2026" — [DPLA PDF](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-English.pdf).
- Age rating definitions: [App Store Connect Help, "Age ratings values and definitions"](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions/) (no date on page; iOS 26-era 13+/16+/18+ system).

Labels: **VERIFIED** = exact quote from the primary source. **INTERPRETATION** = my reading, not legal advice. **SECONDARY** = developer anecdotes, trade press.

The four mechanics (Similar Club): (1) Şanslı hediye = lucky gift: sender buys a gift with coins, chance returns N× coins to the sender, host earns a share; (2) random rewards: loot boxes, spins, Oda roketi random prizes; (3) Oyun Salonu / Rüzgâr Gülü: slots and wheels played with purchased coins; (4) host income: gift → diamonds → cash via platform or agencies.

## Q1. Clause-by-clause: what each Apple clause requires and how it applies to the four mechanics

### Takeaway
No Apple clause bans gifts, tips or creator cash-outs; 3.1.1 expressly allows IAP currency tips to "digital content providers". The deciding line is in the age-rating definitions: wagering with "in-game currency that may be exchanged for real money" is **Gambling** (18+, then 5.3.3/5.3.4 licensing, geo-restriction, no IAP), while wagering without such currency is **Simulated Gambling** (13+ infrequent / 18+ frequent). So the design question for (1)–(3) is whether any chance-derived value can reach the cash-out path in (4). Separately, every randomized paid item needs odds shown before purchase, and all of it must be visible to App Review (2.3.1(a), 2.5.19, DPLA 11.2(g)).

### Cited Findings

**3.1.1 In-App Purchase (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase."
- "Apps may use in-app purchase currencies to enable customers to “tip” the developer or digital content providers in the app."
- "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire, and you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases."
- "Apps may enable gifting of items that are eligible for in-app purchase to others. Such gifts may only be refunded to the original purchaser and may not be exchanged."
- "Apps offering “loot boxes” or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase."
- "Digital gift cards, certificates, vouchers, and coupons which can be redeemed for digital goods or services can only be sold in your app using in-app purchase."
- Application (INTERPRETATION):
  - Coins: IAP only; no expiry for purchased coins. A coin "expiry" or "inactive account sweep" on IAP coins conflicts with this sentence. Free/bonus coins are not named, but mixing them with bought coins in one balance makes the rule hard to show compliance with; keep lots separate (Similar Club already has coin lots).
  - Mechanic (1) lucky gift: a paid action whose outcome is a randomized virtual item/amount is an "other mechanism that provide[s] randomized virtual items for purchase", so the odds of each return multiple must be shown "prior to purchase" (on the gift panel before send, not only in a rules page reached after).
  - Mechanic (2): each loot box, paid spin and paid rocket entry with random prizes needs per-item odds before purchase. A rocket prize funded only by free participation is not "for purchase", but if fuel is bought with coins it is (INTERPRETATION).
  - Mechanic (4): the tip sentence covers coin gifts to hosts ("digital content providers"). The gifting sentence ("may not be exchanged") is about gifting IAP-eligible items; read literally, a gifted item should not be swapped for something else. Converting a received gift into a separate earnings unit (diamonds) is the industry model and Apple publishes no statement against it, but the wording is an ambiguity to be aware of (INTERPRETATION; no Apple clarification found).
- Gift cards: if "Jeton Noktası" or agents sell coin codes/vouchers redeemable in the app, Apple's text says those "can only be sold in your app using in-app purchase", and outside sales may not be advertised in the app outside the US storefront (3.1.1(a), below) (INTERPRETATION, depends on how Jeton Noktası works).

**3.1.1(a) Link to Other Purchase Methods (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "Developers may apply for entitlements to provide a link in their app to a permitted destination in order to purchase digital content or services. These entitlements are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps."
- "The entitlements are limited to use only in specific storefronts of the App Store ... In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront, where this prohibition does not apply, apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase."
- "If your app engages in misleading marketing practices, scams, or fraud in relation to the entitlement, your app will be removed from the App Store and you may be removed from the Apple Developer Program."
- Application (INTERPRETATION): in the Türkiye storefront, no in-app button/link/text sending users to buy coins from a website, agent, WhatsApp seller or "coin trader". Phase 1 found no Türkiye entitlement.

**3.1.3 and 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.3 intro: "Apps in this section cannot, within the app, encourage users to use a purchasing method other than in-app purchase, except for apps on the United States storefront and as set forth in 3.1.1(a) and 3.1.3(a). Developers can send communications outside of the app to their user base about purchasing methods other than in-app purchase."
- 3.1.3(b): "Apps that operate across multiple platforms may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site, including consumable items in multi-platform games, provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app."
- 3.1.3(d): "One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase." (Person-to-person 1:1 services may use other methods.)
- Application (INTERPRETATION): coins bought on Android/web (or from agents) may be spent on iOS only if the same coins are also sold via IAP, and the iOS app may not point to the cheaper channel (non-US). Paid rooms or paid live performances to many listeners are "one-to-many", so IAP. This does not touch cash-outs (money flowing to hosts), which are outside IAP and not a purchase.

**3.2.1(vii) person-to-person monetary gifts (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "(vii) Apps may enable individual users to give a monetary gift to another individual without using in-app purchase, provided that (a) the gift is a completely optional choice by the giver, and (b) 100% of the funds go to the receiver of the gift. However, a gift that is connected to or associated at any point in time with receiving digital content or services must use in-app purchase."
- Scope (INTERPRETATION, fairly strong from the text): this is an exception allowing **real-money gifts outside IAP** (card, Apple Pay, bank). The "100%" condition is the price of skipping IAP. It does **not** govern coin gifts bought via IAP; those are covered by 3.1.1's tip sentence, which has no 100% condition, so a platform/agency share of coin tips is not barred by 3.2.1(vii). The second sentence closes the loophole: a gift to a host during a live room is "associated ... with receiving digital content or services", so it must go through IAP coins, not a direct card payment. Practical consequence: Similar Club cannot let users pay hosts directly by card/bank inside the iOS app to avoid IAP; and a "real-money gift" feature that takes a fee would fail (b).
- 3.2.1(viii): "Apps used for financial trading, investing, or money management should be submitted by the financial institution performing such services and must have necessary licensing ...". Not a cash-out rule, but a wallet-like feature (balances in TRY, transfers between users) would draw this and 5.1.1(ix) (INTERPRETATION).
- 3.2.1(iii): "... all other items and services may not expire."

**3.2.2 Unacceptable (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Current list contains no clause on chance mechanics or creator cash-outs; (ii) and (vi) are "Intentionally omitted". Relevant items: "(v) Arbitrarily restricting who may use the app, such as by location or carrier."; "(x) Apps must not force users to rate the app ... Apps may otherwise incentivize users to take specific actions within apps (such as by completing a level or watching an ad)."; "(iv) ... collecting funds within the app for charities and fundraisers" only for approved nonprofits.
- Application (INTERPRETATION): (v) means feature-gating by country needs a real reason (law, licence). It cannot be used to switch chance features off where reviewers or regulators are and on elsewhere; that would also be undisclosed differential functionality under 2.3.1(a)/2.5.19.

**5.3 Gaming, Gambling, and Lotteries (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Intro: "Only include this functionality if you’ve fully vetted your legal obligations everywhere you make your app available and are prepared for extra time during the review process."
- 5.3.1 "Sweepstakes and contests must be sponsored by the developer of the app."
- 5.3.2 "Official rules for sweepstakes, contests, and raffles must be presented in the app and make clear that Apple is not a sponsor or involved in the activity in any manner."
- 5.3.3 "Apps may not use in-app purchase to purchase credit or currency for use in conjunction with real money gaming of any kind."
- 5.3.4 "Apps that offer real money gaming (such as sports betting, poker, casino games, horse racing) or lotteries must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store. ... Lottery apps must have consideration, chance, and a prize."
- Version history (SECONDARY + VERIFIED): the 2017 text of 5.3.3 also said "... and may not enable people to purchase lottery or raffle tickets or initiate fund transfers in the app" ([Apple forum quoting it, Sep 2017](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/88156)); TechCrunch reported on 7 June 2022 that Apple "removed rule 5.3.3, which had restricted users from buying lottery or raffle tickets or initiating fund transfers in an app" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2331517)). The October 2026 page has only the first sentence (VERIFIED by reading the page), so TechCrunch's "removed 5.3.3" means the second half was dropped, not the whole rule. Some 2026 blogs still quote the old text; do not rely on them.
- Application (INTERPRETATION):
  - Mechanic (3) slots/wheels with IAP coins: if the coins (or their winnings) "may be exchanged for real money" (directly or via the diamond cash-out), this is "real money gaming"; 5.3.3 bars IAP for its credit and 5.3.4 needs a licence in every location where it is used. In Türkiye online casino-type games cannot be licensed to a private operator (phase 1, S5), so this variant cannot be made compliant on iOS for TR.
  - If no path to real money exists, it is not RMG and 5.3.3/5.3.4 do not apply; the obligations become odds disclosure (3.1.1), honest age rating (Simulated Gambling, 2.3.6) and local law (5 intro: "Apps must comply with all legal requirements in any location where you make them available").
  - Mechanic (2) rocket/raffle with real-world prizes (phones, cash, gold): a "sweepstakes"/"raffle" → developer-sponsored (5.3.1), official rules in-app with the Apple disclaimer (5.3.2); if entry is bought ("consideration") and the prize is chance-based, Apple's own "Lottery ... consideration, chance, and a prize" test is met → 5.3.4 licence (INTERPRETATION). Leaderboard prizes (gift rankings, PK) are "contests": 5.3.1/5.3.2 apply, plus the "Contests" age descriptor.

**5.1.1(ix) regulated fields (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "Apps that provide services in highly regulated fields (such as banking and financial services, healthcare, gambling, legal cannabis use, air travel and crypto exchanges) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer."
- Application: an RMG product would have to be submitted by the licensed entity itself (not a white-label publisher). Similar Club's UK company submitting a TR gambling product would need to be the licence holder (INTERPRETATION).

**DPLA (VERIFIED)** — [DPLA LYL262, 29 Oct 2026](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-English.pdf)
- §3.3.4(A)(vi): "Your Application may include promotional sweepstake or contest functionality provided that You are the sole sponsor of the promotion and that You and Your Application comply with any applicable laws and fulfill any applicable registration requirements in the country, territory, or region where You make Your Application available and the promotion is open. You agree that You are solely responsible for any promotion and any prize, and also agree to clearly state in binding official rules for each promotion that Apple is not a sponsor of, or responsible for conducting, the promotion."
  - Note: in Türkiye, prize promotions ("çekiliş") need permission from Milli Piyango İdaresi (outside this note; see legal notes). The DPLA makes "applicable registration requirements" a contractual obligation to Apple, not just a guideline (INTERPRETATION).
- §11.2(g) termination: Apple may terminate "if You engage, or encourage others to engage, in any misleading, fraudulent, improper, unlawful or dishonest act relating to this Agreement, including, but not limited to, misrepresenting the nature of Your Application (e.g., hiding or trying to hide functionality from Apple’s review, falsifying consumer reviews for Your Application, engaging in payment fraud, etc.)."
- §3.2(a)–(b): "You will use the Apple Software and any services only for the purposes and in the manner expressly permitted by this Agreement and in accordance with all applicable laws and regulations;" / "You will not use the Apple Software or any Apple Services: (1) for any unlawful or illegal activity, nor to develop any Covered Product or Corresponding Products, which would commit or facilitate the commission of a crime, or other tortious, unlawful or illegal act". Unlicensed gambling in a market where it is a crime (TR) falls under this contractually (INTERPRETATION).
- Schedule 1 §6.3: "Apple reserves the right to cease marketing, offering, and allowing download by end-users of the Licensed Applications at any time, with or without cause ... if Apple reasonably believes ... that: ... (iii) the distribution and/or use of those Licensed Applications violates any applicable law in any region You designate ... (iv) You have violated the terms of the Agreement, this Schedule 1, or other documentation including without limitation the App Review [Guidelines]".
- I found no DPLA clause that uses "real money gaming" or "gambling" by name (grep of full text for "gambl", "lotter", "real money" returned none). Gambling is governed contractually only through the sweepstakes clause, the legality clauses and the Guidelines.

**2.3.1 / 2.5.19 / 2.3.6 / 2.3.12 hidden features and honest metadata (VERIFIED; rule and consequence only)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 2.3.1(a): "All new features, functionality, and product changes must be described with specificity in the Notes for Review section of App Store Connect (generic descriptions will be rejected) and accessible for review."
- 2.5.19: "Don’t include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features in your app; your app’s functionality should be clear to end users and App Review. Attempting to hide undocumented features in your app binary, such as through code obfuscation or other methods, is grounds for removal of your app from the App Store and termination of your developer account."
- Apple's 16 Oct 2026 explanation of 2.5.19: "This rule clarifies our longstanding prohibition on apps misrepresenting what they do during the App Review process and then changing after approval — for example, adding features or functionality that weren't included in the version of the app that was submitted to App Review. Apps found engaging in this conduct are subject to removal from the App Store and removal from the Apple Developer Program." — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=vvzx6cul)
- 2.3.6: "Answer the age rating questions in App Store Connect honestly ... If your app is mis-rated, customers might be surprised by what they get, or it could trigger an inquiry from government regulators."
- Application (INTERPRETATION): Similar Club changes much behaviour server-side (Canlı ayarlar, Yönetim tables). Under 2.5.19 as clarified, turning on a chance feature (new game, new lucky multiplier tiers, cash prizes) after approval that was not in the reviewed build/notes is exactly the described violation. Every chance feature must be live and reachable in the review build, described in Review Notes, and reflected in the age rating; adding one later means a new submission with notes. Consequence: removal of the app and termination of the account (2.5.19, DPLA 11.2(g)).

**4.7 mini games (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "You are responsible for all such software offered in your app, including ensuring that such software complies with these Guidelines and all applicable laws. Software that does not comply with one or more guidelines will lead to the rejection of your app."
- 4.7.1: must "follow Guideline 3.1 in order to offer digital goods or services to end users" and include filtering, reporting, blocking. 4.7.4: "You must provide an index of software and metadata available in your app." 4.7.5: "a way for users to identify software that exceeds the app’s age rating, and use an age restriction mechanism based on verified or declared age".
- Application: if Oyun Salonu games are HTML5 loaded from the server (or a third-party game provider SDK), each game is Similar Club's responsibility, must use IAP coins, appear in an in-app index with universal links, and be age-gated if above the app's rating. A third-party "game hall" vendor that brings its own slot catalogue does not move responsibility (INTERPRETATION).

**1.2 / 1.2.1 UGC and creator content (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 1.2.1: "Such creator content may include video, articles, audio, and even casual games. The App Store supports apps offering such user-generated content so long as they follow all Guidelines, including Guideline 1.2 ... and Guideline 3.1.1 for payments and in-app purchases. You should communicate to users which content requires additional purchases." 1.2.1(a): age restriction mechanism "based on verified or declared age".
- 1.2: "Developers who fail to implement appropriate safeguards or fail to remediate identified violations after notice may be considered in violation of the Developer Code of Conduct." (16 Oct 2026 addition covers nonconsensual intimate imagery incl. AI-generated — [Apple News](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=vvzx6cul).)
- Application (INTERPRETATION): hosts are "creators"; gift income for live audio is the 1.2.1 model and needs IAP. Hosts running their own wheels/"games" in rooms with real-world side deals would make the app responsible under 1.2 moderation duties.

**5.6 Developer Code of Conduct (VERIFIED)** — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- "Apps should never prey on users or attempt to rip off customers, trick them into making unwanted purchases ... or engage in any other manipulative practices within or outside of the app." / "Your Developer Program account will be terminated if you engage in activities or actions that are not in accordance with the Developer Code of Conduct."
- Application (INTERPRETATION): "first deposit double rewards", near-miss animations, undisclosed odds changes or VIP-dependent odds are the kind of practice that can be cited under 5.6 and 3.1.1 odds disclosure (odds shown must be the odds used).

**Age rating system (VERIFIED)** — [Age ratings values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions/)
- Definitions: "Gambling: Betting or wagering using real money or in-game currency that may be exchanged for real money. May include: casino or card games, sports and non-sports betting, or lotteries and raffles." / "Simulated Gambling: Betting or wagering without using real money or in-game currency that can be exchanged for real money." / "Contests: Events that allow users to compete with one another for rankings, rewards, or the achievement of personal goals. May include: skill-based competitions, trivia quizzes, or sport or fitness contests" / "Loot Boxes: Virtual containers that provide players with randomized virtual items for purchase. May include: randomized functional cards or cosmetic items for purchase."
- Rating table (global): 4+ includes "Infrequent contests", "Messaging and chat", "User-generated content"; 9+ includes "Loot boxes"; 13+ includes "Infrequent simulated gambling", "Frequent contests", "Social media"; 16+ includes "Unrestricted web access"; 18+ includes "Gambling", "Frequent simulated gambling", "Dating and Relationships". Australia: loot boxes 16+, infrequent simulated gambling "R 18+". Brazil: fixed-odds betting needs an SPA licence ("Review App Review Guideline 5.3.4"). No Türkiye regional rule listed (pages list Australia, Brazil, Korea, Vietnam).
- In-App Controls definitions: "Age Assurance: Mechanism to confirm an individual's age meets the age requirement ... May include: declared age range API; age estimation capabilities; age verification via government-issued passport, drivers license, national ID ...".
- Questionnaire / override — [Set an app age rating](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/set-an-app-age-rating/): "This questionnaire includes a list of content descriptors, in-app controls, and capabilities that allow you to specify the frequency of each content type in your app." / "if you believe your app should be rated higher, you can adjust the rating ... by choosing ... Override to Higher Age Rating." / "If your app has a EULA with minimum age requirements that exceed the rating that Apple calculated, you must override to a rating that adheres to the requirements." / "the content descriptions will still reflect your questionnaire answers."
- Application (INTERPRETATION): Similar Club's terms say 18+, so it **must** override to 18+ even if the calculated rating is lower (EULA rule). Overriding to 18+ does not replace honest descriptors: the store page will still show "Simulated Gambling" (or "Gambling") and "Loot Boxes" as answered. Slots/wheels/lucky gifts used daily are "Frequent" simulated gambling. Answering "Gambling" = admitting real-money convertibility, which then triggers 5.3.3/5.3.4.

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION) — mechanic-by-mechanic summary
| Mechanic | Clauses | Compliant on iOS if | Non-compliant if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (1) Lucky gift (N× coin return to sender, host share) | 3.1.1 odds; age "Simulated Gambling"/"Loot Boxes"; 5.3.3/5.3.4 if convertible | Odds per multiplier shown on the gift panel before send; returned value is not convertible to real money (separate non-transferable, non-giftable points, or cosmetics); host share fixed per gift price, not dependent on the random outcome; Frequent Simulated Gambling declared; 18+ | Returned coins can be re-gifted to hosts whose diamonds are cashed out → "in-game currency that may be exchanged for real money" → Gambling + IAP coins (5.3.3 breach); odds hidden or shown after purchase |
| (2) Loot boxes, paid spins, rocket random prizes | 3.1.1 odds; Loot Boxes descriptor; 5.3.1/5.3.2 + DPLA 3.3.4(A)(vi) for real-world prizes | Virtual cosmetic/functional prizes only, odds before purchase; real-world prizes only as free-entry developer-sponsored promotions with in-app official rules and Apple disclaimer, plus local permit | Paid entry + chance + real-world/cash prize = lottery (5.3.4 licence); prizes in coins that feed cash-out |
| (3) Slots, wheels, game hall with purchased coins | 5.3.3, 5.3.4, 5.1.1(ix), 4.7, age rating | Closed-loop play currency with no path to cash or to giftable coins; Frequent Simulated Gambling → 18+; each HTML5 game indexed and age-gated | Any cash-out path from winnings (direct, via gifting, via agencies buying coins back) → RMG; not licensable in TR |
| (4) Host income gift → diamonds → cash | 3.1.1 tip sentence, 1.2.1, 3.2.1(vii) not applicable to IAP coins | Coins bought via IAP; payouts outside Apple; diamonds earned only from gifts at a fixed rate | Direct card gifts to hosts inside the app (3.2.1(vii) second sentence → must be IAP); in-app CTAs to buy coins from agents/web (3.1.1(a), non-US) |

### Gaps
- Apple has no published text on whether "may be exchanged for real money" covers indirect exchange via a third party (host/agency). The definition is not limited to the bettor's own exchange; the reading that it covers the chain is mine.
- I did not find an Apple definition of "real money gaming" beyond the 5.3.4 examples.
- I did not verify whether App Store Connect's questionnaire asks a separate yes/no "Does your app contain gambling?" with help text; the help page describes frequency selections only.

## Q2. Does Apple treat in-app coins as exchangeable for real money when the exchange is indirect (gift → host → cash)? Evidence of how reviewers apply the rules

### Takeaway
No Apple statement addresses the indirect chain. Apple's forums contain only developer opinions; one developer (2023) reports repeated 3.1.1 rejections of a "buy coins → gift creators → creators cash out via PayPal" model with no resolution, while TikTok, Bigo and others run the same model live. Published Chinese rejections show reviewers enforce 5.3.2 (Apple disclaimer in rules) for in-app draws and look for review "switches". The risk from reviewers is real but under-documented; the stronger risk is post-approval enforcement and regulators.

### Cited Findings
- (SECONDARY) Apple Developer Forums thread "Virtual coins and cash out" (Dec 2019): developer asks about "Users can buy virtual coins and use them to tip other users (In-app purchase) / Users can also cash out their coins (via paypal)". No Apple staff reply. A developer reply: "You might point out to App Review that you believe you are operating consistent with Guideline 3.2.1 (vii) and ... that your use of PayPal is only to allow a user to cash out the IAP-purchase (or gifted) Virtual Coins, not to purchase Virtual Coins". Jan 2023 reply (BLAAMFAM): "Our app allows users to buy "BLAAM COINS" they can gift to other creators and the gifted creator can cash out via PayPal. This is EXACTLY how TikTok works. But they keep rejecting our app for guideline section 3.1.1 ..." Outcome unknown. — [Apple Developer Forums 126848](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/126848)
- (SECONDARY) Forum thread on "real money gaming" (Sep 2017), no Apple reply; a developer: "What you describe as "not a game of chance" and "game of skill" is still real money gaming" for entry-fee competitions with cash prizes. — [Apple Developer Forums 88156](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/88156)
- (SECONDARY, rejection text) Chinese developer blog, 14 May 2021: "Guideline 5.3.2 - Legal - Gaming, Gambling, and Lotteries ... Your app includes a contest or sweepstakes but it does not: ... Indicate that Apple is not involved in any way with the contest or sweepstakes." Fix: added the Apple-not-sponsor line to rules always reachable in app, set age rating to 17+, replied to the reviewer with a screenshot. — [cnblogs](https://www.cnblogs.com/ljcgood66/p/14767427.html)
- (SECONDARY, search summary of Chinese posts, not fully read) A developer whose draw page offered third-party memberships as prizes was rejected under 3.1.1; Chinese guides list "2.3.1 – hidden or undocumented features, including hidden 'switches' that redirect to gambling or lottery sites" as a reviewed item; a voice-live app was rejected for danmaku without a report function (1.2). — [CSDN](https://blog.csdn.net/shubinniu/article/details/79142160), [CSDN 3.1.1](https://blog.csdn.net/q1664532726/article/details/115167588), [Juejin](https://juejin.cn/post/6933098071467425799). Note: some of these posts describe hiding draw entries during review as a workaround; that is the conduct 2.5.19 and DPLA 11.2(g) sanction and is not an option.
- (SECONDARY) A Chinese post on a paid voice-chat app reports Apple required removal of paid-companion code and allowed only voluntary gifting — [CSDN 104700431](https://blog.csdn.net/weixin_44076695/article/details/104700431) (page returned HTTP 521; content known only from search snippet).
- (VERIFIED) Apple's 2.3.6 says mis-rating "could trigger an inquiry from government regulators" — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- (VERIFIED, competitor self-description) 101 Okey Yalla (TR storefront) states: "We solemnly declare that: 101 Okey Yalla does not involve gambling with real money, in-game prizes are completely virtual." — [App Store TR](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/id1544887147). Large operators frame chance features as "virtual-only" precisely because that is the Simulated Gambling line.

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION)
- Reviewers see the build and Review Notes, not the agency payout pipeline. A lucky gift whose winnings are re-giftable is therefore unlikely to be caught at first review if not described; but if described honestly (as 2.3.1(a) requires) a reviewer applying the definition would see "in-game currency that may be exchanged for real money". Non-disclosure is not an option (2.5.19, DPLA 11.2(g)); so the only durable path is a design where the honest description is "winnings are not convertible".
- The 2023 forum report shows reviewers can reject even the plain tip-and-cash-out model under 3.1.1 when the flow is unclear (e.g., if cash-out looks like an off-IAP purchase path). Clear Review Notes ("coins only via IAP; hosts are paid from earnings outside the app; no in-app link to other purchase methods") reduce that risk.
- Apple's US social-casino litigation exposure (phase 1, S4) gives Apple a reason to scrutinise convertible chance mechanics more over time.

### Gaps
- No published App Review rejection text naming "lucky gift" / "幸运礼物" was found in English or Chinese sources reachable today.
- No Apple statement, WWDC session or forum answer by Apple staff on indirect convertibility was found.

## Q5. Compliant design variants under Apple rules

### Takeaway
Apple-compliant variants all keep chance away from anything with cash value: (a) chance outcomes paid only in non-transferable, non-giftable points or cosmetics; (b) odds shown on the purchase surface before the tap; (c) host income a fixed share of the gift's list price, independent of the random outcome; (d) honest Frequent Simulated Gambling + Loot Boxes declarations, 18+ override and age assurance; (e) every feature in the review build and notes. Real-money gaming is only possible as a separate, licensed, geo-restricted, free app without IAP submitted by the licence holder, and in Türkiye that licence is not available to a private operator.

### Cited Findings
- Basis clauses (VERIFIED, quoted above): 3.1.1 odds "prior to purchase" and no expiry; definitions of Gambling vs Simulated Gambling; 5.3.3/5.3.4; 5.1.1(ix); 5.3.1/5.3.2 and DPLA §3.3.4(A)(vi); 2.3.1(a)/2.5.19; set-an-age-rating override and EULA rule — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions/), [Set an age rating](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/set-an-app-age-rating/), [DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/apple-developer-program/Apple-Developer-Program-License-Agreement-English.pdf)
- Market practice of "virtual-only" framing: 101 Okey Yalla declaration "in-game prizes are completely virtual" — [App Store TR](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/id1544887147)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION) — variants per mechanic
1. **Lucky gift**
   - Compliant: the gift is bought with IAP coins at a fixed price; the host receives diamonds at a fixed rate of that price (not of the random return); the random return to the sender is paid in a separate "luck points"/game-point unit that cannot be gifted, transferred, traded or converted to coins or diamonds, and does not expire if it came from purchased value (conservative reading of 3.1.1); odds of every multiplier shown on the gift sheet before send, matching the server table; declared as Loot Boxes + Frequent Simulated Gambling; 18+.
   - Weaker variant: return paid as cosmetic items (frames, entry effects) with disclosed odds — Loot Boxes only.
   - Not compliant: return paid in regular coins that can be re-gifted to hosts who cash out (chance value reaches real money → Gambling → 5.3.3 breach with IAP coins).
2. **Random rewards (loot boxes, spins, rocket)**
   - Compliant: prizes are virtual items/points from (1); odds before purchase; rocket rewards funded by the platform, distributed by chance, only in non-convertible units; any real-world prize event is a free-entry promotion with in-app official rules saying Apple is not a sponsor, developer as sole sponsor, local permit (TR: Milli Piyango, see legal notes).
   - Not compliant: paid entry + chance + cash/phones/gold or convertible coins (lottery test in 5.3.4).
3. **Slots / wheels / game hall**
   - Compliant: a closed play currency (bought via IAP is allowed, but winnings stay in that currency and never become giftable coins or diamonds); no cash-out, no agent buy-back; Frequent Simulated Gambling → 18+; for HTML5 games, an in-app index (4.7.4) and age gate (4.7.5); every game present at review and named in notes.
   - Safer variant: remove paid slots entirely, keep skill games/contests (Contests descriptor, 5.3.1/5.3.2 for prizes).
   - RMG variant (theoretical): separate app, licence in each location, geo-restricted, free download, no IAP, submitted by the licensed entity (5.3.3, 5.3.4, 5.1.1(ix)); in TR not available to private operators — so not a real option for the TR market.
4. **Host income / cash-out**
   - Compliant: coins only via IAP (no in-app CTA to agent/web coin sellers in TR storefront); diamonds only from gifts at a fixed, published rate; payout outside the app by the company/agency; no direct card gifts to hosts inside the app (3.2.1(vii) second sentence); Review Notes that describe the flow plainly.
   - Firewall: diamonds must not be fed by chance outcomes (lucky multipliers, game winnings, rocket prizes). If an agency or "Jeton Noktası" buys users' coins for cash, coins themselves become "exchangeable for real money" and every chance feature using coins tips into Gambling.
5. **Review hygiene (rule + consequence only)**: describe each chance feature, its odds and the non-convertibility in Review Notes (2.3.1(a)); ship new chance features only through a reviewed build (2.5.19 as clarified 16 Oct 2026); answer the questionnaire to match real frequency (2.3.6). Breach consequence: removal and account termination (2.5.19, DPLA 11.2(g), 5.6).

### Gaps
- Apple has not said whether a "luck points" unit that can buy cosmetics but not coins is enough; this rests on the definition text only.
- Whether Apple treats a platform-funded random rocket payout in coins (no purchase by the winner) as "for purchase" is not addressed; conservative reading treats it as linked to purchase if fuel is bought.
- Whether Turkish law treats non-convertible simulated gambling as lawful is a legal question outside this Apple-only note.
