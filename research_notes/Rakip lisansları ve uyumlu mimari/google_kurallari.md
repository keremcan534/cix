# Google Play rules for social voice-room apps: lucky gifts, random rewards, in-room slots/wheels, host cash-outs (as of 25 Nov 2026)

Scope: Google only. Builds on phase 1 (`research_notes/Rakip analizi ve yasal yayın stratejisi/magaza_politikalari.md`) and does not repeat its Apple material. All Google pages below were downloaded on 25 Nov 2026 as raw HTML and the quotes are copied from the page text (not from a summariser).
Labels: **VERIFIED** = exact quote from the primary page. **INTERPRETATION** = my reading, not legal advice. **SECONDARY** = press, law-firm or developer blog.

Page dates (VERIFIED where stated): Google Play policy pages carry no "last updated" line (footer only "©2026 Google"). Dated items: RMG pilots page "Effective date: June 4, 2026"; Developer Distribution Agreement "Effective as of September 15, 2025"; Google Ads gambling update "Posted on July 23, 2026" (effective Aug 26, 2026); latest Play policy announcement Oct 29, 2026 (no gambling content). Play policy history for these topics (VERIFIED, [Play policy announcements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/announcements/13412212?hl=en)): May 2019 "We've updated our Payments policy to introduce a requirement to disclose odds for lootboxes. We have updated our Gambling policy with additional examples."; Nov 2019 "We've updated our Monetization and Ads policy on in-app virtual currencies."; Jan 28, 2020 RMG country expansion; Mar 31, 2021 "adding clarifications to the Gamified Loyalty program policy requirements"; Nov 2, 2022 pilots moved to Help Center; Oct 30, 2025 Age-Restricted policy; Jul 15, 2026 anonymous/random chat changes. **No 2024–2026 Play announcement changed the gambling, loot-box or virtual-currency text.**

## Q1. What each relevant Google Play policy says (exact text) and what it requires

### Takeaway
Google's core rule is one sentence: outside a licensed, Google-approved gambling app, nothing may let users "wager, stake, or participate using real money (including in-app items purchased with money) to obtain a prize of real world monetary value". Licensed RMG apps must be free, AO-rated, geo-fenced, age-blocked and must not use Play Billing; Turkey is on the country list, but only for a licensed or governmental operator. "Simulated gambling" has no stand-alone Play policy page: it shows up only in the IARC ratings (Teen), the Families ban, and the ban on gambling ads in social-casino apps. Randomised items bought with money need odds shown before purchase. Play has no clause on creator tipping or creator payouts.

### Cited Findings

**1. Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests** — [policy 9877032](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- Summary: "While Google Play generally prohibits apps from facilitating real-money gambling and gaming, an exception is made for those that are licensed and approved by Google. All approved apps must be free to download, must not use In-app Billing, and must prevent access for minors and unauthorized locations."
- Licensed route: allowed "in select countries, as long as the Developer completes the application process for gambling apps being distributed on Google Play, is an approved governmental operator and/or is registered as a licensed operator with the appropriate governmental gambling authority in the specified country, and provides a valid operating license in the specified country for the type of online gambling product they want to offer." Product types: "Online Casino games; Sports Betting; Horse Racing (where regulated and licensed separately from Sports Betting); Lotteries; Daily Fantasy Sports".
- Requirements include: "Developer must not offer a type of gambling product that exceeds the scope of its gambling license"; "App must NOT be purchasable as a paid app on Google Play, nor use Google Play In-app Billing"; "App must be rated AO (Adult Only) or IARC equivalent"; "App and its app listing must clearly display information about responsible gambling." The application form is linked from "completes the application process" (support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/contact/gambling).
- **Other Real-Money Games, Contests, and Tournament Apps (the key clause):** "For all other apps which do not meet the eligibility requirements for gambling apps noted above and are not included in the "Other Real-Money Game Pilots" noted below, we don't allow content or services that enable or facilitate users' ability to wager, stake, or participate using real money (including in-app items purchased with money) to obtain a prize of real world monetary value. This includes but is not limited to, online casinos, sports betting, lotteries, and games that accept money and offer prizes of cash or other real world value (except programs permitted under the Gamified Loyalty Programs requirements described below)."
- Examples of violations: "Games that accept money in exchange for an opportunity to win a physical or monetary prize"; "Apps that have navigational elements or features (for example, menu items, tabs, buttons, webviews, etc.) that provide a "call to action" to wager, stake, or participate in real-money games, contests or tournaments using real money..."; "Apps that accept or manage wagers, in-app currencies, winnings, or deposits in order to gamble for, or obtain a physical or monetary prize."
- The phrase "prize of real world monetary value" is **not defined** anywhere on the page.
- **Gamified Loyalty Programs, all apps:** benefits "must be clearly supplementary and subordinate to any qualifying monetary transaction within the app (where the qualifying monetary transaction must be a genuine separate transaction to provide goods or services independent of the loyalty program) and may not be subject to purchase nor tied to any mode of exchange otherwise in violation of the Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests policy restrictions." "For example, no portion of the qualifying monetary transaction may represent a fee or wager to participate in the loyalty program..."
- Game apps: "Loyalty points or rewards ... may only be awarded and redeemed on a fixed ratio basis, where the ratio is documented conspicuously in the app and also within the publicly available official rules for the program, and the earning of benefits or redemptive value may not be wagered, awarded or exponentiated by game performance or chance-based outcomes."
- Non-game apps: "Loyalty points or rewards may be tied to a contest or chance based outcomes if they fulfill the requirements noted below": publish official rules in the app; for chance-based rewards disclose "1) the odds for any reward programs which use fixed odds ... and 2) the selection method"; "Specify a fixed number of winners, fixed entry deadline, and prize award date, per promotion" for drawings and sweepstakes; document any fixed ratio. Table: Game = chance-based loyalty "Not Allowed", fixed ratio "Allowed"; Non-Game = both "Allowed", T&Cs and odds "Required".
- Gambling ads inside apps are allowed only if, among others, "App must not provide simulated gambling content (for example, social casino apps; apps with virtual slot machines)" and "App must not be enrolled in the Designed for Families program". Violation example: "A simulated casino game that promotes or directs users to real money casinos".

- Countries listed (Nov 2026): Australia, Austria, Belgium, Brazil, Bulgaria, Canada, Colombia, Croatia, Czech Republic, Denmark, Ethiopia, Finland, France, Germany, Ghana, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Kenya, Latvia, Lithuania, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Nigeria, Norway, Panama, Peru, Philippines, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Serbia, Singapore, Slovakia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Tanzania, Turkey, Ukraine, United Kingdom, United States. - Phase-1 note: Google Ads' Turkey entry says "Operator must be a state licensed entity" ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6018017?hl=en)). INTERPRETATION: Google lists Turkey, but the licence has to come from Turkish law, and Turkish law gives none to a private online-casino operator (see the legal notes). So a UK company running a social app cannot use this route for Turkey.

**3. Real-money pilots** — [Help 12918670](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918670?hl=en) (VERIFIED, "Effective date: June 4, 2026")
- "There are currently no active pilots on Google Play."
- India: "As of January 22, 2026, the pilot grace period is no longer in effect. Apps that were previously in the pilot can only remain on Google Play in India after this date if they remove any real-money functionality".

**4. Simulated gambling / IARC content rating** — [Content ratings 188189](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/188189?hl=en); [policy 9898843](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898843?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- Policy: "All apps must have a content rating from the IARC to be on Google Play. You must accurately complete the Play Console questionnaire for every app, and keep it updated if content in the app changes".
- Re-rating: the questionnaire must be redone for "All app updates where there has been a change to your content or features that would affect the responses to the questionnaire".
- "Misrepresentation of your app's content may result in its removal or suspension." "App updates and submissions can be rejected for misrepresenting an app's content."
- Rating text: Teen: "May contain violence, suggestive themes, crude humor, minimal blood, simulated gambling and/or infrequent use of strong language." Adults Only 18+: "May include ... gambling with real currency." USK/PEGI-style rows also list "simulated gambling" at the 12-level band ("...as well as nudity of a slightly more graphic nature and simulated gambling").
- Families: "Apps that include real or simulated gambling" are not allowed in apps targeting children ([Families 9893335](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en), phase 1).
- There is **no** Play policy requiring an age gate for simulated gambling. The Age-Restricted policy (below) names only RMG, dating and random/anonymous chat.
- There is **no** Play text saying that simulated currency must not be redeemable. The "no real-world value" requirement for social casino comes from **Google Ads**: "Social casino games are online simulated gambling-style games where there is no opportunity to win something of value." and certification requires "statements that they aren't offering or promoting real money gambling and are not providing prizes of real-world value." ([Ads 6018017](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6018017?hl=en)). On Play, that boundary is drawn by the RMG clause itself (a "prize of real world monetary value" = RMG).
- SECONDARY: IARC adds the interactive element "In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)" for loot boxes. A peer-reviewed study found that this label could not be treated as reliable proof of loot boxes on Google Play ([Xiao et al., Royal Society Open Science 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10049760/)).

**5. Payments** — [policy 9858738](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- Play Billing is required for in-app purchases, for example "Items (such as virtual currencies, extra lives, additional playtime, add-on items, characters, and avatars)".
- Play Billing "must not be used in cases where: ... payments include peer-to-peer payments, online auctions, and tax exempt donations; payment is for content or services that facilitate online gambling, as described in the Gambling Apps section of the Real-Money Gambling, Games, and Contests policy".
- Anti-steering: apps "may not lead users to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system" via the listing, "In-app webviews, buttons, links, messaging, advertisements, or other calls to action", or sign-up flows (Sections 8 and 9 alternative-billing programs only in eligible countries).
- "In-app virtual currencies must only be used within the app or game title for which they were purchased."
- "Apps and games offering mechanisms to receive randomized virtual items from a purchase including, but not limited to, "loot boxes" must clearly disclose the odds of receiving those items in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, that purchase."
- "Developers must clearly and accurately inform users about the terms and pricing of their app or any in-app features..."
- **There is no creator-tipping or creator-payout clause.** The words tip, gift, creator and payout do not appear in the policy text. Unlike Apple 3.1.1 ("tip ... digital content providers"), Google never says tipping creators is allowed or forbidden. It falls under the general rule: coins bought through Play Billing, spent inside the app.

**6. Behavior Transparency (Deceptive Behavior §5) and Deceptive Behavior** — [policy 9888077](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077?hl=en) (VERIFIED; rules and consequences only)
- "Your app's functionality should be reasonably clear to users; don't include any hidden, dormant, or undocumented features within your app. Techniques to evade app reviews are not allowed."
- Key Considerations: "Make sure your app behaves identically for a regular user and for a Google Play reviewer."; "Don't dynamically download and execute code from a remote source that introduces features or functionality not present during the app review."; "Don't use techniques to detect the app review environment and change your app's behavior to hide non-compliant features."; "Don't show different functionality based on user geography or device parameters, or other user-dependent data without prominently advertising it."
- "Attempts to manipulate Play review processes, including misleading reviewers as to the purpose and functionality of your apps, are strictly prohibited." "Any claim that an app is a "prank", "for entertainment purposes" (or other synonym) does not exempt an app from application of our policies."
- Consequences: DDA 8.3 and 10.3 below (removal, suspension, withheld payments, account termination).

**7. Device and Network Abuse** — [policy 9888379](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- "an app may not download executable code (such as dex, JAR, .so files) from a source other than Google Play. This restriction does not apply to code that runs in a virtual machine or an interpreter where either provides indirect access to Android APIs (such as JavaScript in a webview or browser)."
- "Apps or third-party code, like SDKs, with interpreted languages (JavaScript, Python, Lua, etc.) loaded at run time (for example, not packaged with the app) must not allow potential violations of Google Play policies."
- Violation: "a webview with added JavaScript Interface that loads untrusted web content (for example, http:// URL) or unverified URLs".
- INTERPRETATION: Server-loaded HTML5 games are technically allowed. But a game added after review that brings a new chance feature conflicts with the Behavior Transparency line "features or functionality not present during the app review". Each new game must be compliant by itself and declared (store listing, IARC answers).

**8. User Generated Content** — [policy 9876937](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- "You must provide clear terms of use, an in-app system for reporting objectionable content, and a function for users to block others. Incidental sexual content must be filtered by default and restricted from minors."
- Required: "Provides safeguards to prevent in-app monetization from encouraging objectionable user behavior." INTERPRETATION: gift-for-attention economies (hosts paid by gifts) sit directly under this line. Chamet was removed for UGC, not gambling (Q4).

**9. Child Safety Standards** — [Help 14747720](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14747720?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- "We require Anonymous and Random Chat apps, and apps in the Social and Dating categories to comply with our Child Safety Standards policy." Requirements: published CSAE standards (a globally reachable web page linked in Play Console), "an in-app mechanism for user feedback", address CSAM, comply with child-safety laws, "Provide a child safety point of contact" (named in Play Console). "The presence or absence of child users in your app is irrelevant to this policy."

**10. Age-Restricted Content and Functionality** — [policy 16302250](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16302250?hl=en) (VERIFIED; Oct 30, 2025, expanded Jul 15, 2026)
- "apps meeting the descriptions below are required to use the Play Console functionality and tools to block minors: Apps that facilitate Real Money Gambling, Games, and Contests. Apps whose core functionality is to facilitate matchmaking or dating. Apps where matchmaking or dating is an incidental feature are not required to block minors, provided they implement appropriate age-gating safeguards to prevent minors from accessing those specific features. Apps whose core functionality is to facilitate randomly connecting users to spontaneously communicate with people they do not know. Apps whose core functionality is for users to communicate anonymously where the identity of one or more users is deliberately hidden."
- July 15, 2026 announcement: "our Age-Restricted Content and Functionality and Child Safety Standards policies have new requirements and restrictions for anonymous chat and random chat apps. Our Families Policy Requirements policy also now prohibits anonymous chat apps from targeting children." ([Announcements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/announcements/13412212?hl=en))
- INTERPRETATION: Simulated gambling alone does not trigger the "block minors" tool. If Google decides the coin economy is RMG, the tool becomes mandatory, but the app would still be RMG without a licence, which a Play Console age block does not fix. A voice app with a "random match" feature as core function also falls in.

**11. Financial features declaration** — [Help 13849271](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271?hl=en) (VERIFIED)
- "All developers that have an app published on Google Play must complete the Financial features declaration, including apps on closed testing, open testing, or production tracks." Categories include "Payments and transfers: Mobile payments and digital wallets; Money transfer and wire services", crypto wallet and exchange. Apps without financial features must declare that. "the information that you provided will be reviewed by Google as part of the app review process."
- INTERPRETATION: The declaration has no "creator payouts" category. An in-app cash-withdrawal flow for hosts (bank or IBAN payout) is closest to "money transfer". Answer accurately, and keep payouts outside the APK (panel or agency) if that reflects how they really work. Unresolved: Gaps.

**12. Google Play Developer Distribution Agreement** — [DDA](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html) ("Effective as of September 15, 2025") (VERIFIED)
- 4.1: "You and Your Product(s) must adhere to the Developer Program Policies."
- 8.3: if a Product "(a) violates any applicable law; (b) violates this Agreement, applicable policies ...; or (d) creates potential liability for, or may have an adverse impact on, Google ... then Google may reject, remove, suspend, limit the visibility of a Product on Google Play, or reclassify the Product ... If Your Product is rejected, removed, or suspended ... then Google may withhold payments due to Developer."
- 10.3: Google may terminate immediately if "(a) You have breached any provision of this Agreement ..." or "(e) You or Your Product pose a potential risk for economic, reputational, or security-related harm to Google, users, or other third-party partners."
- 14.1: the developer indemnifies Google against third-party claims arising from "(c) You or Your Product(s)' violation of any law." Note: that is exactly what the US social-casino suits ask of the platforms (Q4).
- 16.8: California law, Santa Clara courts.
- 3.8: Google may grant refunds and deduct them from payouts.

### Inferences
- INTERPRETATION: For a non-licensed app, Google's only test is whether money (or items bought with money) is staked on chance for a prize of real-world monetary value. Everything else (age gate, IARC, odds disclosure) is secondary. "Simulated" is safe on Play only while the prize side carries no real-world monetary value.
- INTERPRETATION: The Payments clause "must not be used ... for content or services that facilitate online gambling" means a finding of RMG breaks the coin sale itself, not just the games: Google could treat every coin IAP as Play Billing used for gambling.

### Gaps
- The exact wording of the IARC questionnaire questions on gambling and simulated gambling in Play Console could not be retrieved. They are behind the Console login and no primary public copy exists. Only Google's rating-band descriptions are verified.
- No Google page defines "real world monetary value".

## Q2. Does Google treat coins as having "real world monetary value" if they can be gifted to a host who cashes out?

### Takeaway
No Google Play text, Help Center example, Play Academy page or 2024–2026 announcement addresses the "gift to host, host cashes out" chain directly. The Play text points to "yes, risky" in three places: "in-app items purchased with money" count as real money on the stake side; "Apps that accept or manage ... in-app currencies, winnings ... in order to ... obtain a ... monetary prize" is a listed violation; and Google Ads (same company, same definitions) calls games played with "virtual currencies or items that have real-world value" online gambling. The verdict is INTERPRETATION.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED stake side: "participate using real money (including in-app items purchased with money)" — [9877032](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en). Coins bought through Play Billing are therefore "real money" for the wager test.
- VERIFIED prize side: "to obtain a prize of real world monetary value"; violations "Apps that accept or manage wagers, in-app currencies, winnings, or deposits in order to gamble for, or obtain a physical or monetary prize." — [9877032](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- VERIFIED Google Ads definitions: gambling = "Staking something of value on the outcome of events or processes determined by an element of chance with the opportunity to win something of value"; online gambling examples: "online gambling games played with virtual currencies or items that have real-world value"; "Social casino games are online simulated gambling-style games where there is no opportunity to win something of value."; "Examples of games that are not social casino games: Sweepstake casinos" — [Ads 6018017](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6018017?hl=en)
- VERIFIED Google Ads Aug 26, 2026 update (posted Jul 23, 2026): new certification forms for online gambling, "online non-casino games (where certification is required) and social casino games"; "A single Google Ads account cannot hold both an online gambling certification and a social casino game certification simultaneously." — [Ads 17258294](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17258294?hl=en). Google keeps the two categories strictly apart.
- VERIFIED NFT analogy (phase 1): "anything of monetary value should not be accepted in exchange for a chance to obtain an NFT of unknown value"; violation "Pay-to-play social casino games, such as slot machines, that reward NFTs" — [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16302285?hl=en). Google treats a transferable or tradable prize from paid chance as the problem.
- VERIFIED Payments: "In-app virtual currencies must only be used within the app or game title for which they were purchased." — [9858738](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- SECONDARY developer report (2020): Google rejected an app update with "Issue: Violation of Gambling policy". The fix that got it approved: "Remove all features which offers cash prizes/gift/cash equivalent and resubmit" — [technuisance.com](https://technuisance.com/google-play/android-app-update-rejected-issue-violation-of-gambling-policy-solved.html). A quiz/reward app, not a voice app; Google treated "gift/cash equivalent" prizes as gambling.
- SECONDARY legal analogy: US courts and the Washington AG treat even non-cashable virtual chips as a "thing of value" (Kater v. Churchill Downs, 9th Cir. 2018; WA AG release, Feb 3, 2026: the release cites "a 2018 Ninth Circuit ruling that virtual currency in casino apps are a 'thing of value'") — [WA AG](https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/ag-s-office-sues-illegal-gambling-apps-have-taken-more-225-million)

### Inferences
- INTERPRETATION: In Similar Club the chain is: Play-bought coins → chance (lucky-gift multiplier, wheel, slot, rocket) → coins → gift → host diamonds → cash through the agency or a withdrawal. The coins won by chance can reach cash one step later through another user. Google's text doesn't say the cash-out has to be by the winner. "Apps that ... manage ... in-app currencies, winnings ... to ... obtain a ... monetary prize" is the closest description. A reviewer would likely read the prize as having "real world monetary value". The risk is highest where the winner and the host collude (self-gifting to an alt or partner host account), which turns the chance into a direct cash prize.
- INTERPRETATION: The host's share of a plain (non-random) gift is not a chance outcome. It is a fixed-ratio conversion of a purchase, close to the creator-economy model (TikTok LIVE, Bigo) that Play tolerates. Google has no clause against it. The risk comes only from connecting a chance layer to the same currency that cashes out.
- INTERPRETATION: If coins move outside Play Billing (coin dealers or resellers paid in cash, a "Jeton Noktası" style resale), two more Payments issues arise: steering to another payment method ("In-app ... buttons, links, messaging ... or other calls to action") and coins gaining an outside market price, which strengthens the "real world monetary value" reading.

### Gaps
- No Google statement, Help Center example, Play Academy lesson or PolicyBytes video on creator gifting with cash-out, or on lucky gifts, was found.
- Google never publishes reviewer guidance, so how a reviewer handles the "indirect cash-out through another user" case cannot be confirmed.

## Q3. How the rules apply to the four mechanics, and compliant design variants under Google rules

### Takeaway
Under Google's text, each of the four mechanics is allowed by itself: random rewards with odds disclosure, simulated slots and wheels with an accurate IARC rating, host gift income with platform payouts. The prohibited configuration is the combination: chance staked with purchased coins, paying out in the currency that a host can cash out. Compliant variants cut that link. Without a licence, which Turkey does not give a private operator, there is no Google-approved way to keep chance outcomes cash-convertible.

### Cited Findings (rules applied; texts as quoted in Q1)
- Loot box/odds: "randomized virtual items from a purchase ... must clearly disclose the odds ... in advance of, and in close and timely proximity to, that purchase" — [Payments](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en)
- RMG ban: "wager, stake, or participate using real money (including in-app items purchased with money) to obtain a prize of real world monetary value" — [RMG](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Loyalty (game apps): "fixed ratio basis ... may not be wagered, awarded or exponentiated by game performance or chance-based outcomes"; loyalty rewards "may not be subject to purchase" and no part of the transaction may be "a fee or wager" — [RMG](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Simulated gambling is a Teen-band rating element; real-currency gambling is AO — [188189](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/188189?hl=en)
- Licensed RMG cannot be added to an existing app: "Existing app cannot be repurposed for real-money gambling ... you must upload your new real-money gambling app bundle or APK as a new app with a new package name." — [Help 13381106](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13381106?hl=en) (phase 1)

### Inferences (INTERPRETATION) — mechanic by mechanic

**(1) Lucky gift** (sender buys a gift with coins, host gets a fixed share, sender randomly gets back 0–N× coins)
- It is a "randomized virtual item from a purchase", so odds must be shown before each send (multiplier table and probabilities, shown next to the send button).
- When the returned coins can be re-gifted to a host who cashes out, it matches the RMG clause: the stake is purchased coins, the prize has cash value, the outcome is chance. It also resembles "exponentiated by ... chance-based outcomes" in the loyalty rule. It cannot count as a loyalty program, because the purchase itself is the wager.
- Compliant variants: (a) the return goes into a separate non-transferable, non-giftable, non-cashable points balance (cosmetics, badges, entry to simulated games only) and the host share stays a fixed ratio of the gift price; (b) the random part is purely cosmetic (animation, badge, effect tier) with no currency return; (c) remove the random return entirely. In every variant: odds shown before purchase, and the IARC answers updated.

**(2) Random rewards** (loot boxes, spins, room-rocket random prizes)
- Bought with coins → odds disclosure is required. Free (earned by activity, no purchase) → the Payments odds rule does not strictly apply, but disclosing is the safe practice. If the prize is cashable coins or diamonds, the RMG clause applies whenever the entry was paid (the rocket is fuelled by gifts, i.e. by purchased coins).
- Compliant: prizes in non-cashable points or cosmetic items. Room-rocket prizes must not credit host diamonds or giftable coins, or the rocket must be funded and paid without a chance element (fixed reward per contribution, documented ratio).

**(3) In-room slots, wheels and game halls played with purchased coins**
- If winnings are paid in giftable or cashable coins: unlicensed RMG → rejection or removal, and Play Billing for these coins is then "for content or services that facilitate online gambling".
- Compliant closed-loop "social casino" variant: a separate game currency (bought through Play Billing or earned) whose winnings stay in that currency and can never be gifted, transferred, converted to coins or diamonds, or cashed out. IARC questionnaire answered "simulated gambling" (Teen band or higher; Similar Club is 18+ anyway). No gambling ads in the app ("must not provide simulated gambling content" if showing gambling ads). Not in Families. Games served as HTML5 in a WebView are fine under Device and Network Abuse, but new games must be in place and declared at review time ("Don't dynamically download and execute code ... not present during the app review"). Optional and stricter: play only with earned (non-purchased) currency.
- Google requires no age gate for simulated gambling, but the 18+ position and Play Console's age tools are still advisable (Turkish law; Families exclusion).

**(4) Host or creator income from gifts, cashed out by the platform or agencies**
- No Play clause forbids it. Coins sold via Play Billing, spent in the app (gifts), host earnings in a separate unit (diamonds) that the developer pays out outside Play. The financial features declaration must be accurate; UGC "safeguards to prevent in-app monetization from encouraging objectionable user behavior" apply; CSAE standards apply (Social category).
- Compliant conditions: a deterministic, documented host share; diamonds earned only from non-random gift value (exclude lucky-gift returns and game winnings); no coin resale or steering outside Play Billing; anti-collusion controls (self-gifting, agency-owned alt accounts), because collusion turns any chance mechanic into a cash prize.

**(5) The licensed RMG route**
- Only for a licensed or governmental operator, in a listed country, as a **new app with a new package name**, AO rating, free, no Play Billing, geo-blocked, minors blocked, responsible-gambling info. Turkey is listed, but no private online-casino licence exists there (INTERPRETATION, see legal notes). The UK is listed (UKGC licence), but that would be a separate UK-only gambling app, not Similar Club.

**Not an option:** turning features on only after review, or only for some countries or users without disclosure. This is explicitly prohibited (Behavior Transparency) and leads to removal or termination under DDA 8.3/10.3.

### Gaps
- Google publishes no "approved design" examples for social-casino or voice apps. The variants above are derived from the text, not confirmed by Google.

## Q4. How Google actually enforces these rules; enforcement and litigation (status Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
No public, verifiable case was found of Google removing a voice-chat app specifically for lucky gifts or in-room slots. Voice and live-chat apps with lucky wheels remain listed. Known removals of live and voice apps were for UGC (Chamet, 2023) or ad fraud (StarMaker, 2025). Google's enforcement is mostly invisible: 1.75 million apps rejected and 80,000+ developer accounts banned in 2025. The US social-casino MDL against Apple, Google and Meta is still on Section 230 appeal at the 9th Circuit (briefing and argument Nov 2026). The Washington AG sued social-casino operators, not Google, in Feb 2026.

### Inferences
- INTERPRETATION: Google's enforcement against this genre is opaque and inconsistent. Similar apps stay listed for years, then a single complaint, press story or regulator request triggers removal. Under DDA 8.3(d) ("adverse economic, reputational ... impact") Google can act without citing a specific gambling clause. With the platforms themselves being sued over social-casino payments, Google's incentive to police "real-world value" links is rising.
- INTERPRETATION: The real enforcement risk for a small new developer is higher than for incumbents: one strike can terminate the account (and linked accounts), withhold payouts (DDA 8.3), and Google keeps refund rights (DDA 3.8).
