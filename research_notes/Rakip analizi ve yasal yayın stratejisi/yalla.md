## 1. What the filings say about virtual items, "Gold"/"Diamonds", host incentives, withdrawals and cash redemption

### Takeaway
Yalla's audited filings say flatly that no virtual currency or gift can ever be turned into real money, sold between users, refunded, or used to make money, and that this is a deliberate platform design choice ("not to make money"). The filings disclose no cash payouts to hosts or room owners. They say Yalla has no significant "incentive fees to key users", a structural difference from host- and agency-payout apps.

### Cited findings
- **No cash conversion, in the business description.** "While our users may receive virtual gifts and accumulate virtual currencies on our platform, none of the virtual gifts and virtual currencies are convertible into real money under any circumstance and our users are unable to sell such virtual items to other users through our platform. Instead, users send virtual gifts to express their appreciation of contributions by other users or gain recognition within the community." — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Monetization section, the key passage.** "Users buy our virtual currencies through third-party payment platforms. Our virtual currencies are non-refundable and do not have expiration date. We believe our users come to our platform primarily for an enjoyable social experience, and not to make money. We have designed our platform settings to ensure that our users cannot use our platform for money-making purposes. On Yalla, while our users may receive gifts from other users, such gifts cannot be redeemed into real money. Similarly, on Yalla Ludo, while our users may win virtual currencies from other users that they play games with, they can only use such virtual currencies to play more games and/or gain advantages in games. Our users are not allowed to convert any of our virtual currencies into real money or sell our virtual currencies to other users under any circumstances." — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **What users spend currency on.** "Individual users consume virtual currencies to purchase virtual items and upgrade services or play games on our platform. Virtual items primarily consist of various virtual gifts and privileges in chat rooms or games. Upgrade services primarily consist of VIP rights or premium membership on our platform." — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Games charge an entry fee in virtual currency.** "Individual users consume virtual currencies to play a game, purchase virtual items which can be used to enjoy one time privileges in the game, and upgrade service. Each game is completed within several minutes. The Group charges individual users virtual currencies to play a game, which currently accounts for a small portion of games service revenue." — [20-F FY2025, Note 2(m) Revenue Recognition](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Accounting treatment.** Revenue from virtual items is recognized "at the point-in-time when they are consumed". VIP and upgrade revenue is recognized ratably over the service period. Money paid for virtual currency is booked as **deferred revenue (a contract liability)**: US$58.1M at 1 Jan 2025, of which US$55.8M was recognized as revenue during 2025. — [20-F FY2025, Note 2(m)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **No significant payouts to hosts or key users.** "Our innovative business model focuses on users' interactions and social networking experience on our platform. Therefore, we do not incur significant content acquisition cost, such as incentive fees to key users or costs for acquiring media content." Cost of revenues "consists primarily of (i) commission fees paid to third party payment platforms and (ii) staff cost and expenses related to the operations of our mobile platform." — [20-F FY2025, Item 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Recognition takes the place of cash.** "To incentivize gifting, we have created rankings to recognize users who have given the most gifts, as well as users who have received the most gifts, and we refresh these rankings on a real-time basis." Elsewhere: "We incentivize user spending by recognizing their generosity in the Yalla community." — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Room roles.** The filing describes a room "chair" (the owner) and "ministers" (moderators): "Room chairs and ministers are incentivized to maintain a healthy environment in their rooms to attract and retain members. They have the ability to remove users from their rooms for rule violations." The incentive is not specified as monetary. — [20-F FY2025, Content Management and Monitoring](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm). The same wording appears in the [F-1 (2020)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312520240880/d850602df1.htm).
- **"Lucky Golds" chance-style sharing.** "A user can also share virtual currencies with others under the 'Lucky Golds' feature to bring excitement to the room." This is the filings' only description of a luck-based distribution feature. It appears in both the [F-1 (2020)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312520240880/d850602df1.htm) and the [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm). The 20-F FY2025 full text contains no occurrences of "lottery", "lucky draw", "loot box" or "chance".
- **Currency names.** The filings never name the currencies; the text has no matches for "gold coins" or "diamond". The consumer terms do: the Yalla Jackaroo Terms of Service (Yalla Technology FZ-LLC, Dubai Internet City) refer to "Virtual Currency (such as Golds and Diamonds in our Platform)". — [Yalla Jackaroo ToS](https://www.yallajackaroo.com/agreements/en/termsService.html)
- **Consumer terms, §12 "Virtual Goods and Virtual Currency".** "Virtual Currency do not represent any credit balance of 'real world money' or the equivalent, and cannot be redeemed for 'real world money' or anything of monetary value. You acknowledge that you do not own the Virtual Items… All sales of Virtual Items by us to you are final. You will not trade, sell, gift, transfer or lend Virtual Items (a 'Virtual Transaction'), except as expressly permitted by us in writing. Any user… who violates this restriction may have his or her account terminated… have Virtual Goods and/or Virtual Currency forfeited." The same terms say: "no Virtual Goods and Virtual Currency shall be refundable after purchase (whether used or unused) under any circumstances." They also forbid users to "Gamble, provide gambling information or induce others to gamble in any way." The terms refer to a separate "Virtual Items Policy" and "Community Guidelines". — [Yalla Jackaroo ToS](https://www.yallajackaroo.com/agreements/en/termsService.html)
- **Yalla Ludo terms, per a third-party summary only.** Diamonds and Gold are non-refundable and cannot be traded, sold, gifted, transferred or lent. The ToS was "last updated July 22, 2026" and the Virtual Items Policy was updated on 2025-08-08. This is a secondary source; the official text was not retrieved. — [buffget.com summary](https://buffget.com/news/yalla-ludo-terms-of-service-2026-refunds-bans-account-deletion-age-rules)
- **Real-world value reward in a sister app.** The YallaChat Google Play description says "Play Ludo, plant your date-palm tree to get mobile credits". Mobile airtime is a real-world-value reward. — [Google Play listing text, fetched 2026-11-25](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yalla.yallalite&hl=en&gl=TR). The text appeared inside the Yalla Lite page as YallaChat's description.

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION: Yalla's legal and store-safety position rests on a "closed-loop" economy: currency goes in, nothing comes out, and users cannot trade it. The filing language reads like it was drafted with gambling and money-transmission definitions in mind, since the "consideration + chance + prize" test needs a prize of monetary value.
- ASSUMPTION: Yalla Ludo lets users "win virtual currencies from other users" in paid-entry games, which is a stake-and-win pattern. Yalla's stated defence is that the winnings cannot leave the platform. The store ratings in section 3 show the stores still label 101 Okey Yalla "Simulated Gambling".
- ASSUMPTION: "No significant incentive fees to key users" means that, at least at the level disclosed, Yalla does not run a host/agency cash-payout economy like Bigo/Poppo-style apps, where diamonds received are exchanged for money. If such payouts exist, they are immaterial or are not described as such.

### Gaps
- No filing or official document found describes a host or agency salary, diamond-to-cash exchange, or withdrawal function in the Yalla app. Whether some unofficial or in-app host scheme exists in practice could not be verified from public official sources. Accounts and app inspection were out of scope.
- The full official Yalla (yalla.live) user agreement could not be read. The page `https://apiv2.yalla.live/web/Agreement.html?type=N`, linked from the App Store, is a JavaScript single-page app with no server-rendered text. The Jackaroo ToS from the same Dubai entity was used as a proxy.
- The Virtual Items Policy text and the exact Gold/Diamond roles in Yalla (as opposed to Yalla Ludo) are not documented.

## 2. Risk factors on gambling, chance features, app store policies and payment channels (including offline recharge agents)

### Takeaway
The risk factors treat gambling mainly through two lenses: (a) content and use of the platform for "gambling purposes" being banned in MENA, and (b) payment and virtual-currency rules (money transmission, prepaid access, AML, gambling) that "may be unclear". They do not name Apple or Google. They describe all purchases as going "through third-party payment platforms". They disclose no offline recharge agents or coin resellers, although an authorized web reseller (Coda) exists outside the app stores.

### Cited findings
- **Gambling as prohibited use (content risk).** "The regulations in some countries in MENA and Southeast Asia prohibit online social networking platforms from being used for dating, pornographic or gambling purposes. While we do not believe our mobile applications are provided to users for any of these purposes, we cannot control how users interact online or offline other than through content monitoring on our mobile applications. We may be subject to fines or other disciplinary actions…" — [20-F FY2025, Risk Factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Payments and virtual-currency regulatory risk.** The risk factor heading reads: "Users' payments to purchase and use of virtual currencies on our mobile applications could expose us to additional regulatory requirements…" The text continues: "We may be subject to a variety of laws and regulations in the various jurisdictions where our users are located in respect of the users' payments to purchase virtual currencies on our applications through third-party payment platforms, including those governing money transmission, gift cards and other prepaid access instruments, electronic funds transfers, anti-money laundering, counter-terrorist financing, gambling, banking and lending. In some jurisdictions, the application or interpretation of these laws and regulations may be unclear… we may be subject to monetary fines or other penalties, or we may be required to make product or marketing practice changes… potential fraudulent or otherwise illegal activity by users, employees, or third parties." — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Dependence on payment platforms and their fees.** "Currently, we process purchases of our virtual currencies through third-party payment platforms… there are currently only a limited number of reputable third-party payment systems in our target markets. If any of these major payment systems decides to cease to provide services to us, or significantly increases the fee rates it charges us for using its payment systems for our virtual currencies, our results of operations may be materially and adversely affected." — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Platform risk without naming the stores.** "We are dependent on the effective interoperation of our mobile applications with mobile operating systems, such as Android and iOS… Any changes… that degrade the functionality of our mobile applications or give preferential treatment to competitive products could adversely affect usage." The 20-F FY2025 text contains zero occurrences of "Apple" or "Google Play"; Google appears only as "Google Ads". — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **UAE content law and gambling.** The TDRA (Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority) "has the authority to require the blocking of any website involving inappropriate content, such as pornography, nudity, illegal drugs or gambling". Federal Decree Law No. 34 of 2020 (Rumours and Cybercrimes) lists "gambling" among offending content and carries criminal exposure. Article 53 fines run from AED300,000 to AED10M for failing to remove illegal content. Article 58 extends liability to de facto managers. There is "no safe harbor defenses in UAE law". — [20-F FY2025, Regulation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Auditor's check of payment flows.** In its critical audit matter, KPMG "compared the amounts recharged by the users for purchasing virtual currencies against the statements of third-party payment platforms and the amounts of cash received by the Company". This shows recharges are reconciled to payment-platform statements. — [20-F FY2025, auditor's report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Official web store outside the app stores.** The Yalla Ludo web store says: "THE OFFICIAL YALLA LUDO WEB STORE IS OPERATED BY CODA" and "CODA IS AN AUTHORIZED RESELLER OF DIGITAL CONTENT FOR YALLA LUDO" (Coda Payments Pte. Ltd.). It sells Diamonds and Gold in USD-denominated packs, for example USD 10 = 5,150 Diamonds or 1,463,320 Gold, and USD 100 = 55,800 Diamonds. Payment is by card, "no registration, or log-in required", and items are credited to a player ID. — [yallaludo.codashop.com (KW)](https://yallaludo.codashop.com/en-kw/yalla-ludo)
- **Grey-market top-up sellers.** Many third-party top-up sites (Joytify, Bitrefill "Yalla Live gift card", EnjoyGM, LootBar, ar-pay) sell Yalla Gold, Yalla Ludo Gold and Diamonds by user ID. Their authorization status is unverified. — e.g. [Joytify](https://www.joytify.com/en-us/yalla-live-gold), [Bitrefill](https://www.bitrefill.com/lt/ko/상품권/yalla-live-lithuania/), [ar-pay](https://ar-pay.com/en-in/product/yalla-ludo-usd-25-gold)

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION: "Third-party payment platforms" in the filings covers Apple IAP, Google Play Billing and web merchants of record such as Coda. Yalla deliberately avoids naming the stores.
- ASSUMPTION: Yalla's 2025 cost saving, "lower commission fees… as a result of diversified payment channels" (see section 4), most likely reflects moving part of recharges to web or direct channels (such as Coda) that charge lower fees than the stores' ~15–30%. This is an inference; the filing does not say which channels.
- ASSUMPTION: Selling through an authorized reseller that credits a player ID with no login keeps the flow merchant-of-record and auditable, unlike informal cash "coin agents". No disclosure of offline cash agents was found. If they exist, Yalla does not present them as part of its model.
- The filings never say a chance feature is "not gambling". The posture is "users cannot make money, so it isn't". "Lucky Golds" is described neutrally as excitement.

### Gaps
- The filings give no breakdown of revenue by payment channel (IAP vs. web vs. other) and no named payment-processor concentration.
- No disclosure was found on offline recharge agents, cash coin dealers or carrier billing.
- No earnings-call transcript quote on the payment-channel mix was retrieved (search found only press releases).

It is live on the Turkish App Store and Google Play with "Simulated Gambling" descriptors and Google Play PEGI 12. The main Yalla, Yalla Ludo, Yalla Lite and YallaChat apps are also on the Turkish App Store. - ASSUMPTION: Apple's "Loot Boxes" descriptor on the main Yalla voice-chat app implies paid random-reward items (lucky gifts or boxes) exist in the voice-chat app itself, even though the 20-F does not describe them beyond "Lucky Golds". This was not verified in-app (out of scope).
- The 12+ / PEGI 12 / PEGI 3 ratings conflict with the ToS minimum age of 18 for Turkey. Yalla resolves this with parental consent for ages 13–18 rather than an 18+ hard gate. For an 18+ app such as Similar Club, Yalla is not a model for age gating.

### Gaps
- It is unconfirmed whether Yalla Parchis or WeMuslim are available in Turkey; they were not queried individually.
- The Turkish-language store descriptors (as Turkish users see them) and the exact IARC certificate for Turkey were not captured.
- Apple's new 13+/16+/18+ tiers (2025) are not reflected in the lookup API's `contentAdvisoryRating` field, which still shows "12+". The live page may display differently.
- No Turkish regulatory action (BTK, MASAK, Spor Toto/Milli Piyango gambling authority) against Yalla apps was found.

## 4. Revenue split, paying users, ARPPU, payment channels and margin

### Takeaway
In FY2025, chatting earned about 63% of revenue and games about 36%, on US$341.9M total revenue with about 10–12M quarterly paying users and an ARPPU of about US$7–8 per quarter. The largest cost is commissions to payment platforms (cost of revenues was about 33% of revenue). "Diversified payment channels" cut those commissions by US$4.9M in 2025. Net margins stay very high (36% GAAP in Q3 2026).

### Cited findings
- **Revenue by service (Note 14, US$):**

  | Year | Group chatting | Games | Others | Total |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2023 | 211,760,853 | 106,742,196 | 374,515 | 318,877,564 |
  | 2024 | 225,367,240 | 113,639,697 | 668,908 | 339,675,845 |
  | 2025 | 216,386,896 | 124,033,272 | 1,517,942 | 341,938,110 |

  — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Quarterly operating data, 2025 (Q1 → Q4):** average MAUs 44.6M / 42.4M / 43.4M / 44.8M; paying users 11.8M / 11.2M / 11.4M / 10.4M; ARPPU US$6.9 / 7.4 / 7.7 / 7.9. In 2024, paying users were 12.8M / 12.0M / 12.6M / 12.3M and ARPPU was US$6.0 / 6.6 / 6.9 / 7.2. — [20-F FY2025, Item 5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Paying users definition.** Paying users are "registered users who played a game or purchased our virtual items or upgrade services using virtual currencies… at least once in a given period, except for users who receive all of their virtual currencies directly or indirectly from us for free". ARPPU is revenue from main apps divided by paying users. — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Payment-platform promotions drive paying users.** The paying-user decline from 12.3M to 10.4M (Q4 2024 to Q4 2025) was "primarily due to fewer promotional events offered by third-party payment platforms". — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Payment commissions and margin.** "Our cost of revenues decreased by 7.1% from US$120.5 million in 2024 to US$111.9 million in 2025, mainly due to lower commission fees paid to third-party payment platforms by US$4.9 million from 2024 to 2025 as a result of diversified payment channels. Cost of revenues as a percentage of our total revenues decreased from 35.5% in 2024 to 32.7% in 2025." In 2024, commissions rose by US$7.5M with revenue, and cost of revenues was 35.9% of revenue in 2023. — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm). The Q4 2025 press release similarly attributes a 15.1% fall in cost of revenues to lower commissions from diversified payment channels. — [Barchart/PRN Q4 2025 release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/650416/yalla-group-limited-announces-unaudited-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial-results)
- **Q3 2026 (latest, 6-K filed 2026-11-17).**
  - Revenue was US$90.4M (+0.9% YoY): chatting US$50.3M, games US$38.4M (games +13.7% YoY).
  - Net income was US$32.7M (36.2% net margin).
  - Average MAUs were 47.2M and paying users 12.1M (+6.2%).
  - Cost of revenues was 32.1% of revenue, "due to an increase in share-based compensation expenses and higher technical service fees".
  - Selling and marketing rose 73.3% to US$16.6M to promote new games, including a "second SLG title this year" in a publishing push.

  — [6-K Q3 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526464687/yala-ex99_1.htm)

## 5. Corporate structure, domiciles, MENA regulatory risks and disclosed incidents

### Takeaway
Yalla is a Cayman holding company with UAE (Dubai free-zone FZ-LLC) operating companies and PRC subsidiaries for technology, operations and finance. Its key regulatory exposure is UAE VoIP licensing, content/cybercrime law, the new UAE Child Digital Safety law and Saudi licensing uncertainty. No regulator ban was found; the main public "incident" was the 2021 short-seller attack and a dismissed class action.

### Cited findings
- **Structure.** "We are a Cayman Islands holding company and conduct our operations mainly through our operating subsidiaries in the UAE and China." The business launched in April 2016 via FYXTech BVI and Shenzhen Yale and was reorganized in 2018. Operating companies:
  - Yalla Technology FZ-LLC (Yalla UAE, July 2018) and Waha Technology FZ-LLC: "primary business operation center… sales, marketing, customer service".
  - Metaverse Technology FZ-LLC (Meta UAE) and Hangzhou Yale Information: technology and product development.
  - Shenzhen Moov: marketing and financial reporting.
  - YG Technology FZ-LLC: game launching.
  - Shenzhen Moojo and Shenzhen Moojoyo: gaming operational support.
  - Yalla Game Limited (Cayman, 60% owned): mid-core and hard-core games.

  ADSs have been listed on the NYSE since 2020-09-30. — [20-F FY2025, Item 4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Footprint.** Servers are "hosted mainly in data centers in Germany, the United States, Ireland and Mexico". The platform is available in 160+ countries, with Yalla in 8 languages. The company holds 177 registered trademarks. Customer service is outsourced partly to providers in Egypt and the UAE. — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **UAE tax.** Qualifying Free Zone Person 0% / 9% corporate tax, plus a 15% Pillar-2 top-up tax from 2025-01-01 for large multinational groups. — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **VoIP risk.** "Yalla apps' free voice chat function may be deemed VoIP services". Unlicensed VoIP in the UAE is a criminal offence (AED50k–1M fine and/or up to two years' imprisonment for the supplier), and "the main enforcement action… is… to block the VoIP service in the UAE". Saudi law "is unclear about whether we are required or eligible to obtain licenses for… audio social media services, including the… VoIP license". — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Child safety.** "UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 On Child Digital Safety Law came into force on January 1, 2026", with a one-year compliance period; implementing Cabinet decisions were not yet issued as of 2026-03-31. Platforms must "develop and implement enhanced child protection measures". — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **Moderation.** "We are unable to examine the content generated by our users on air before they are streamed." Moderation combines terms of service, "a content monitoring system that utilizes primarily automation, as well as manual screening", operations staff, user reports, and room chairs and ministers who can remove users. — [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- **2021 short-seller attack.** Swan Street and Gotham City Research alleged inflated MAUs and bots in 2021. Yalla said it "has not placed any robots into any of its chatrooms" and announced a US$150M buyback. A shareholder suit followed in August 2021. — [Al Jazeera, 2021-11-01](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2021/11/1/uae-based-yalla-from-high-flyer-to-short-seller-target); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-10-31/-clubhouse-of-the-middle-east-short-sellers-say-user-numbers-are-fake). Yalla later announced dismissal of the class action. — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/yalla-group-announces-dismissal-of-securities-class-action-lawsuit-301464072.html)

### Inferences
- ASSUMPTION: The China-based technical and operations subsidiaries mirror Chinese voice-room practice (gifts, rankings, VIP). Yalla's notable departure is the explicit no-cash-out rule and the absence of paid host agencies.

### Gaps
- No regulator ban, fine or store removal of any Yalla app was found in public sources (search 2026-11).
- No Turkish-specific regulatory disclosure: the 20-F contains no occurrences of "Turkey" or "Türkiye" apart from the product description of 101 Okey Yalla.

## 6. Lessons: what a listed, audited company does to stay within store and gambling rules

### Takeaway
Yalla's playbook combines four elements: a closed-loop virtual economy with no cash-out and no user-to-user trading; recognition rewards (rankings, VIP) instead of cash rewards for hosts; payments only through regulated payment platforms or authorized resellers, reconciled by the auditor; and honest store labelling ("Simulated Gambling", "Loot Boxes") with parental-consent age rules.

### Cited findings (recap of the evidence behind each lesson)
- No conversion to real money and no selling between users "under any circumstances": [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm). ToS §12 says currency "cannot be redeemed for 'real world money' or anything of monetary value", sales are final, and trading or transfer leads to forfeiture: [Yalla Jackaroo ToS](https://www.yallajackaroo.com/agreements/en/termsService.html)
- No significant "incentive fees to key users"; gifting is driven by real-time rankings: [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)
- Purchases go only "through third-party payment platforms", and recharges are audited against platform statements: [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm). The web channel runs through an authorized reseller (Coda) as merchant of record: [Yalla Ludo webstore](https://yallaludo.codashop.com/en-kw/yalla-ludo)
- Store descriptors are self-declared: "Simulated Gambling" (101 Okey Yalla, PEGI 12 / Apple 12+) and "Loot Boxes" (Yalla voice chat): [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yalla.okey101yalla&hl=en&gl=TR), [Apple lookup](https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=1091902866&country=tr)
- Risk factors openly flag gambling, money transmission, prepaid-access and AML laws as "unclear" in some jurisdictions: [20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794350/000119312526168267/yala-20251231.htm)

### Inferences (for Similar Club; labelled as assumptions)
- **ASSUMPTION: cash-out is the key difference.** Similar Club has para çekme (withdrawal), Jeton Noktası (coin point), agency payouts and diamond-to-cash conversion. Yalla's public, audited position is the opposite. Its whole gambling defence ("users cannot use our platform for money-making purposes") would not be available to an app that pays out. The difference matters most where chance features (Şanslı hediye/lucky gift, Oyun Salonu/game hall, Rüzgâr Gülü/wheel, Oda roketi/room rocket) coexist with cash-out. Purchased coins + chance + a cashable prize is the classic gambling triad.
- **ASSUMPTION: chance features can survive at a listed company.** Yalla's chance-style features (Lucky Golds, Ludo/Okey wins with entry fees, Loot Boxes) survive because every prize stays inside the closed loop.
- **ASSUMPTION: Yalla's model is cheaper to run.** It shows a gifting business can earn 36–40% net margins without host salaries, which removes agency-payout, KYC and payout-fraud exposure.
- **ASSUMPTION: Yalla does not answer two Similar Club questions.** A web or reseller recharge channel can cut store fees, but Yalla shows no evidence of offline cash agents. Its ToS age rules (Turkey 18, 13–18 with parental consent) are looser than an 18+ app needs, so Yalla is no benchmark for strict age gating.

### Gaps
- Whether Yalla operates any unofficial or regional host-reward programme in practice was not verified (no accounts or app use).
- No legal opinion or ruling on Yalla's model in Turkey (Law 7258 on betting/gambling, MASAK) was found.
