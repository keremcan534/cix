# Timo (Fastwall) dossier + industry map of agency-based voice/live apps (Turkey & MENA)

All sources accessed 25 Nov 2026 unless another date is given. Reliability labels: **[primary]** = store listing, the company's own documents, regulator or court material, or an SEC filing; **[news]** = mainstream Turkish or international press; **[low]** = user complaint sites, reseller or top-up blogs, agency recruitment content. Nothing here comes from creating an account, installing an app or opening an APK/IPA. The Timo agreement texts were read from the public web page that the Play listing links as its privacy policy (h5.touchchat.me), and the web top-up code from the public website timo.club. ## Q1. Who is behind Timo, where is it registered, what does it offer, how is it gated?

### Takeaway
The "Timo" family active in Turkey is published on Google Play by **Fastwall Information Technology Co., Limited**, a Hong Kong company (Tsim Sha Tsui address). The developer account is named "FAST WALL" and runs three apps: Timo – Chat Near & Real Friend (10M+ installs), **Timo Club** (50M+) and Timo Lite (1M+). All three are rated PEGI 18. The model is paid 1-on-1 video and voice calls plus voice party rooms: "gold coins" are bought, women hosts earn "diamonds", and the published rate is **20,000 diamonds ≈ 1 USD**. Hosts are paid through agencies. Its own Turkish-language host and agency texts and Turkish users' complaints (withdrawals to Akbank, "slot" losses) show it is active in Turkey. Neither the agreements nor the store pages mention any gambling licence. The two Timo iOS apps found earlier now return nothing from Apple in any storefront tested.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The "Timo" the owners mean is very likely Fastwall's **Timo Club** (50M+). Its Turkish agreement texts, Turkish agency complaints and Turkish bank withdrawals all point there. The Chinese comments, the "shejiao" (社交, "social") CDN domain and ThinkingData suggest a team of Chinese origin working through a Hong Kong company. The Hong Kong company was not checked in the HK Companies Registry.
- [INFERENCE] The iOS apps returning 404 everywhere suggests they were taken down, either by Apple or by the developer. The date and reason are unknown. Timo now looks like an **Android-plus-web-top-up business**.
- [INFERENCE] "No data collected" in Data safety does not match location, camera and mic permissions plus an analytics SDK. This is a Google Play policy risk for them, not a model to copy.
- [INFERENCE] Hosts are paid through agencies in USD to local banks (Akbank in Turkey, Easypaisa in Pakistan). Timo pays the agency, and the agency pays the hostess. So Timo has no direct relationship with most hosts, and their KYC and tax handling (e.g. Turkish income tax on these earnings) falls to agencies.
- [INFERENCE] Coin-priced slot games, PEGI 18 without a gambling descriptor, and a crypto pay-in on the web are the same pattern that drew enforcement on other platforms (see Q4). The owners shouldn't read Timo as proof that "this is allowed in Turkey".
- [INFERENCE] Timo was **not** in the reported BTK list of 14 Feb 2026 (Q4), so it may have picked up users and agencies leaving Bigo, SUGO, MICO and Poppo. The Cey Agency complaint ("we brought over 70 broadcasters … from another platform", Nov 2025) came before that ban, so this is not proven.

## Q2. What do the big platforms' official agency/host policies publish about commission, bean/diamond→cash rates, minimum withdrawals, KYC?

### Takeaway
The listed companies say in their filings that they share virtual-item revenue with "performers and talent agencies". They do **not** publish rate cards. The commonly quoted figures (Bigo 210 beans = $1, $31.90 minimum, $5,000 a week cap; Chamet 16,670 diamonds = 10,000 beans, about 60/40, $10 minimum through MetWallet; agency tiers of 5–40%) come almost entirely from top-up resellers' blogs, which disagree with each other. Timo is the rare case that publishes its rate openly (20,000 diamonds ≈ $1).

### Cited Findings
- JOYY (Bigo's parent), FY2025 Form 20-F: "We share a portion of the sales proceeds of virtual items ('revenue sharing fee') with **performers and talent agencies** … Those performers who do not have revenue sharing arrangements with the Group are not entitled to any revenue sharing fee." JOYY books the revenue gross and records the sharing fee as cost of revenue. BIGO segment cost of revenues was **US$1,206.1m in 2025**, down 5.3%, "driven by decrease in revenue-sharing fees". — [JOYY 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1530238/000110465926049917/joyy-20251231x20f.htm) [primary]
- Bigo, third-party figures: "210 Beans = $1 USD", minimum 6,700 Beans (≈$31.90), weekly cap 1,050,000 Beans ($5,000), one withdrawal per 7 days, government ID + SMS verification, the account holder name must match the Bigo name, bank fee "$3 + 2% FX" against Payoneer "$3 + 0.3%", 3–30 days to process. — [Buffget (2026)](https://buffget.com/news/how-to-convert-bigo-beans-to-cash-2026-rates-and-rules), [Buffget Payoneer vs bank](https://buffget.com/news/bigo-live-golden-bean-withdrawal-payoneer-vs-bank-guide), [BitTopup](https://bittopup.com/article/BIGO-Beans-Cashout-Guide-2026-48Hour-Hold-Limits) [low; resellers]
- Bigo agency tiers, third-party: "20-40% commissions … one-time setup fees starting at $145"; another version says 20% for domestic and 30% for international agencies, more for diamond resellers. These sources contradict each other. — [Buffget 2025](https://buffget.com/news/bigo-live-agency-2025-2040-commissions-easy-application-guide), [Buffget 2026](https://buffget.com/news/bigo-live-agency-2026-tiers-diamonds-and-commissions) [low]
- Chamet, third-party: "16,670 Diamonds = 10,000 Beans" (60% host / 40% platform), $10 minimum withdrawal through **MetWallet**, $1.20 fee. Agency tiers start at 5% for $500–1,499 a month and reach 18–24%; another blog claims up to 30%. — [Buffget](https://buffget.com/news/chamet-agency-revenue-model-diamond-to-cash-rates-2026), [BitTopup](https://bittopup.com/article/Chamet-Agency-vs-Solo-Host-2025-Commission-Earnings) [low; conflicting]
- Timo, for comparison: 20,000 diamonds ≈ 1 USD. The agency pays the hostess. Formula: Host Revenue = Calls value × Host Profit Share; Agency Revenue = Host Revenue × Agency Share. — [Timo agreement bundle](https://h5.touchchat.me/social/agreement/index.html#/diamond) [primary]
- Payout rails mentioned for the sector: Payoneer and bank for Bigo [low, above]; MetWallet for Chamet [low, above]; Easypaisa for Timo in Pakistan [low, Play review]; USD to Akbank for Timo in Turkey [low, Şikayetvar]; crypto pay-in on Timo's web recharge [primary, timo.club code]. No official source tied **Papara** to any of these platforms' payouts. Papara's "Streamer Account" is aimed at Twitch-type donations. — [Papara FAQ](https://test.papara.com/en/faq/account/is-the-streamer-account-paid) [primary]

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The sector norm is that the platform keeps about 40–60% of gift value, the host gets 30–60%, and the agency takes a 5–40% override (paid by the platform or out of the host's share). Hosts withdraw in USD with a minimum, a weekly cap and a hold period, after ID checks. Treat any number as indicative only.
- [INFERENCE] Platforms keep agency rate cards private, inside agency dashboards. Commission terms therefore spread through WhatsApp/Telegram recruiters, which is also where fraud and money laundering complaints start.

### Gaps
- No official Bigo, Poppo, SUGO, MICO, Chamet or Tango agency policy page could be found or opened. Official help centres are mostly in-app. Official rate cards, KYC levels and Turkish payout methods are UNKNOWN.
- Tango's and Hello Group's revenue-sharing disclosures were not checked (time limit). Yalla's 20-F risk-factor text on games/virtual currency was not read.

## Q3. How do official "coin seller / recharge agent" programs work and what discount do they get vs IAP?

### Takeaway
No official public page from Bigo, Poppo, SUGO, Chamet or MICO describing a coin-seller program was found. Third-party sources describe an in-app "coin seller"/"coin trading" role: the platform sells coins in bulk to approved resellers, who then move them to users' IDs. Typical claimed savings against app store prices are about 10–25%. Offers of 30–50% or more are widely called scams. Timo's own web "quickCharge" (one account pays, another receives) and its Top-up Agreement warning about "non-platform designated recharge channels" show the same thing: off-store, ID-based top-up.

### Cited Findings
- Poppo: "Poppo itself doesn't have an official reseller program for everyone"; resellers are "official partners or third-party platforms that got authorization". A guide describes an "Activate Coin Seller" step in the Help Center and a "Coin Trading" transfer flow. Neither could be checked against Poppo's own documents. — [Topuplive Poppo coin seller guide](https://www.topuplive.com/news/poppo-coin-seller-guide.html), [BitTopup agency-level pricing "15-25% savings"](https://bittopup.com/article/Unlock-1525-Savings-Poppo-AgencyLevel-Live-Coin-Pricing-Guide-for-Managers) [low]
- Poppo ID top-up rates quoted by a reseller: "8,300–9,460 coins per $1" (2026). — [Buffget](https://buffget.com/news/poppo-live-coins-top-up-8300-9460-coins-per-dollar1-in-2026) [low]
- SUGO: a reseller offers top-up by UID only (no password), with "savings of up to 12%". — [Buffhub SUGO](https://buffhub.com/top-up/sugo.html) [low]
- Reseller blogs warn that discounts above 30–50% outside official events point to scams, banned accounts or lost funds. — [Buffget Poppo](https://buffget.com/news/poppo-live-coins-top-up-cheapest-dollar100-pack-in-3-steps) [low]
- Bigo: no official coin-seller page was found. The official channels named by third parties are feedback@bigo.tv and safety@bigo.tv (for checking an agency). — [Eneba support article](https://eneba.com/support/article/how-to-top-up-bigo-live) [low]
- Timo: the Top-up Agreement places losses from "non-platform designated recharge channels" on the user and says buying "through other channels may have certain commercial risks (including … criminals using your account or bank card to carry out illegal activities)" (Indonesian version). The web rechargeMall supports paying for another account (`payUserId` ≠ `acceptUserId`), per-channel KYC flags and a crypto pay-in. — [Timo agreement bundle](https://h5.touchchat.me/social/agreement/index.html#/recharge); [timo.club rechargeMall](https://timo.club/static/js/rechargeMall.Cy0H0Zsj.js) [primary]

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The reseller discount of about 10–25% against app store prices is roughly the 15–30% app store fee that the platform saves, passed partly to the reseller. Coin sellers are often also agencies, so one party both sells coins and collects host income. That loop (cash in → coins → gifts to own hosts → diamonds → cash out) is exactly the money laundering pattern in the Turkish TikTok case (Q4).
- [INFERENCE] Pay-for-another-account top-up and crypto pay-in increase AML exposure. For Similar Club, this is a launch-blocker type of decision for the owners (see `docs/DENETIM.md` and the cash-out items in `docs/YAYIN_HAZIRLIGI.md` §1).

### Gaps
- No official program terms (eligibility, deposit, volume tiers, discount %, KYC) from any of the named platforms. All discount figures are from resellers. UNKNOWN whether Turkish "jeton bayi" networks for these apps are official.

## Q4. Enforcement events (dates, sources), especially Turkey

### Takeaway
In Turkey, BTK has twice blocked whole groups of these apps: **about 29 video-chat/dating apps (decisions of 20 Aug 2025, approved by Ankara criminal judgeships of peace)** and **9 live-streaming apps on 14 Feb 2026 (Bigo, MICO, SUGO, Poppo, MIGO, SoulChill, LiveMe, Cheero, LopMe)**. No official reason was published for either. Bigo Live, SUGO, LiveMe and LopMe are now absent from the Turkish App Store. Separately, in **November 2026** Ankara prosecutors ran a gendarmerie operation in 8 provinces over **TikTok live-stream gifts allegedly used to launder illegal-betting proceeds**: 11 detained, MASAK found ₺1.93bn moving through the accounts. Payment rails used by betting networks were also hit: Papara was placed under a TMSF trustee (May 2025), and İninal, Payfix and Aypara lost their licences (Nov 2025). Abroad: Pakistan blocked Bigo (Jul 2020), India blocked JOYY apps (2020), Indonesia warned Bigo over gambling and porn (Jul–Aug 2024), Egypt ran a 2025 crackdown on creators that included money laundering charges, and US courts have treated social-casino chips as "a thing of value".

## Q5. Which platforms visibly run slots/wheel/lucky games, and do listed companies avoid them?

### Takeaway
Apple's own descriptors show **"Simulated Gambling"** on Hago and Qwick Live (HAGO Singapore, a JOYY brand), Hawa (Owol Technology), Party Star (Gumdrop Entertainment) and older Ola Party-family listings. Many others (Bigo Live, SUGO, MIGO, Hiya, Azal, SoulStar, Yalla) declare **"Loot Boxes"**. Azal's listing openly describes "virtual diamond slot-style games played with in-app virtual currency only". So listed companies do not avoid these games across the board. JOYY's Hago carries the Simulated Gambling descriptor while Bigo Live declares only Loot Boxes. Timo (unlisted) has coin slots according to users but declares no gambling descriptor.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The working distinction across the industry is **"chance game with purchased coins and no cash-out of winnings"**, declared to Apple as "Simulated Gambling" or "Loot Boxes". It becomes legally dangerous once winnings can be gifted to a host and cashed out as diamonds. That turns it into staking money for a prize of money's worth (cf. Big Fish's "thing of value"). Whether Timo or others allow winnings to reach diamonds is UNKNOWN and is the key question.
- [INFERENCE] The listed groups (JOYY/Bigo, Yalla) seem to keep their flagship streaming apps at "Loot Boxes" and put explicit chance games in separate game apps (Hago, Yalla Ludo). That is a separation by app, not avoidance.
